# Separating saved code quality from worker completion

A fresh local coding pilot used ten small module contracts and hidden semantic checks. Correct and deliberately broken controls qualified the graders before the workload was sealed. Interrupted execution, uncertain ownership and restart recovery also had explicit acceptance boundaries.

8 of ten saved implementations passed every frozen hidden check; 94 of 106 cases received verified credit. Worker outcomes were 8 budget_reached, 1 failed, 1 returned. The pilot used one local worker job per task with unchanged limits and no retries. Saved files were independently checked after each worker outcome. Consecutive matching failures paused for review; remaining fresh work continued without replaying earlier effects.

Supplemental source checks then demonstrated an uncovered string-coercion bug in the snapshot decoder and duplicate-dependency and reserved-name bugs in the dependency module. Its frozen zero score reflected a missing named export, with interface wording insufficiently explicit; that score does not establish eleven semantic failures. The search module also failed duplicate-ID rejection; four self-authored oracle expectations incorrectly treated literal substrings. Original files and fixed-suite scores stayed unchanged. The loader task also rejects null/undefined sync or async failures and remains loading, despite its 12/12 frozen score. Its own test runner credits PASS before awaiting promises and masks an omitted callback TypeError as a loader failure. Source-review shows own negative-oracle sentinel mistakes in three tasks, with a bounded known-nonrejecting control for one of those tasks. These defects remain unrepaired and do not change fixed scores. Passing a frozen suite or an own check therefore does not establish complete contract correctness.

8 tasks produced invalid check arguments, and 6 attempted cleanup through read-only checks. Multiline inline checks arrived as invalid arguments, while short file-based checks could execute. Later cleanup attempts were rejected by read-only isolation. Those observations support a narrower explanation than a broad model-capability verdict; they do not establish that every budget failure had the same cause.

The focused proposal is clearer worker instructions: save a small self-check file, invoke it with a short array of arguments, retain verification files, then return a concise report after successful verification. It would preserve budgets, authority and local inference. This is a proposal only; no production instruction was changed, and a later controlled evaluation would be required before claiming a gain.

Preparation failures were retained: missing APIs initially received incorrect throw credit, and restart and checker-error boundaries needed correction before inference. A missed preceding backup was followed by exact source recovery, while the historical timing gap remained disclosed. A later intermediate report-edit gap retained original backups but lacked independent intermediate fingerprints. A single-step checked backup-and-edit helper then governed remaining updates. Dependent privacy checks were sequenced after clone completion.

The pilot is descriptive and uses a smaller workload than earlier application experiments. No direct comparison, general reliability gain or throughput improvement is claimed. Production behavior remained unchanged, earlier negative experiments stayed negative, and the ordinary supervisor path and physical phone behavior were outside acceptance. The reusable lesson is to inspect saved behavior and exact tool-loop evidence separately before choosing a focused change.
